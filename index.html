Javad Tamnanloo

Ph.D. Candidate, School of Polymer Science and Polymer Engineering, The University of Akron, Akron, OH

I use computational methods to understand and design materials, working with Prof. Mesfin Tsige at the University of Akron. My toolkit combines machine learning, including graph neural networks, with molecular dynamics, density functional theory, and multireference quantum chemistry. Together they connect a material’s chemical structure to how it behaves, from the way molecules pack and move in polymers, at surfaces, and in solution to how chemical bonds break and form under heat and mechanical force.

I work closely with experimental groups, using simulations and machine learning to explain their measurements and to guide which materials to make next. I am an NSF NRT fellow in a graduate traineeship on machine learning for materials science. Selected projects, several with simulation movies, are on my research page, and you can try one of my machine learning models with the surface tension predictor.

Before coming to Akron, I earned my B.S. and M.S. in chemical engineering at the University of Tehran, where I worked on gas adsorption and separation, and then worked as a technology development engineer in pharmaceutical manufacturing. I am currently a data science intern at Bridgestone Americas.
